# correction: marker_trait_attr solves disjunctive SFINAE gating

_2026-08-25 10:14:35_

Corrected a wrong claim in wiki/concepts/cxx-translation-pain-points.md: had
logged disjunctive SFINAE gating (enable_if<A<T>::value || B<T>::value>,
same function body regardless of which predicate matched) as having no Rust
target even under nightly specialization. That's wrong for the
uniform-body case.

#![feature(marker_trait_attr)] is a distinct nightly feature from
specialization, not a variant of it. #[marker] relaxes coherence
specifically for traits with zero items, so overlapping blanket impls
(impl<T: TraitA> PassesFilter for T {}, impl<T: TraitB> PassesFilter for T
{}) compile and correctly gate a bound on A-or-B. Verified: compiles, runs
correctly for A-only/B-only/both, and correctly rejects a type implementing
neither. Also verified the boundary is compiler-enforced (E0714/E0715): a
marker trait or its impls cannot carry any items, which is exactly why it
only covers gating, not dispatch (if constexpr-style differing bodies per
branch still needs ordinary non-overlapping impls, which was never actually
hard).

Full writeup, including the corrected section:
wiki/concepts/cxx-translation-pain-points.md.
