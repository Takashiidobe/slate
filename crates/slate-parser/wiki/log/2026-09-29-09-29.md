# Split long variable-bearing FileCheck lines into -SAME pieces; render fixtures with the release binary

_2026-09-29 09:29:36_

Named IR variables (becf64da) made FileCheck slow on a few fixtures. Any [[...]] or {{...}}
turns a check line into a regex that LLVM compiles per line. Its cost grows with pattern length
times scanned text, and superlinearly with capture groups. cpp__embed-14.c (one line with 256
definitions) went from 1ms to 5.5s. bitint-39.c (20KB constants with one binding each) took 3.7s.
FileCheck has no flag that changes this.

tools/update_filecheck.py gains split_regex_checks. A line over 1024 chars, or with more than 32
constructs, becomes -NEXT plus -SAME pieces. Each piece holds at most one construct and 160 chars
of literal text, so long literal runs are fixed strings again. A first rule (len>160 or >8
constructs) split 80k lines in 4800 fixtures and was rejected as too broad. The final rule splits
821 lines in 228 fixtures. embed-14 now checks in 13ms and bitint-39 in 15ms.
20001226-1.c is untouched: its 16k ~300-char lines are slow only in aggregate, ~1.5s.

The updater also ran every render through cargo run in the debug profile. float-exact-1.c took
9.9s (9.2s of it in the debug binary) and now takes 0.9s. It now builds release once and calls
target/release/slate-parser directly; examples use cargo run --release. The full regeneration
takes 2:36.

Suite: 14.3s -> 11.8s wall, and summed test time 146s -> 130s. What remains is spread across
fixtures: 263k of 508k check lines carry a construct.

Removed 8 harness scratch copies (.X.filecheck.RAND.c) accidentally committed in becf64da. Added
.*.c to .gitignore, and the updater now skips dot-files like the harness does.
