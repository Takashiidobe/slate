# Gate integer literal candidate types on the language standard

_2026-09-18 07:27:13_

`integer_candidates` (src/sema/validate.rs) returned the C99 list in every
standard mode, so `-std=c89` rejected literals clang accepts.

Added `StandardFeatures::long_long_type`: `Standard` from C99 on, `Extension`
in C89 (clang, gcc and msvc all accept `long long` there). The candidate
builder now derives the list from it instead of hardcoding ranks:

- C89 decimal, unsuffixed or `l`: int, long, **unsigned long** (6.1.3.2),
  then the `long long` extension tail.
- C99+ decimal: int, long, long long (6.4.4.1) — no `unsigned long`.
- Every mode: a decimal literal fitting no signed type falls back to the
  unsigned form of the widest rank, matching clang's
  `-Wimplicitly-unsigned-literal`. This was also missing before, and was why
  the reported repro failed under `-std=c99` too.

Threaded `StandardFeatures` into `numeric::Context` (new `with_features`)
so validate's diagnostics and the IR literal typing share one list.

C89 and C99 produce identical IR on LP64 because `long` and `long long` are
both 64-bit; they only diverge where `long` is 32-bit (ILP32, and LLP64 as on
windows-msvc). `long` width is target state (`TargetInfo::long_width`), not
compiler flavor, so the candidate list stays flavor-free.

Verified all 24 (literal, standard) pairs against clang 22 on `-m32`, and the
LP64 boundaries on x86_64; fixtures pin both.

Filed slate-parser-47s.8: we now silently accept two extensions with no
warning channel and no `-Werror`/`-pedantic-errors` handling. Individual `-W`
names do not change generated code, but `-pedantic-errors` does — it must
make `Extension` behave as `Rejected`, dropping the `long long` tail.
