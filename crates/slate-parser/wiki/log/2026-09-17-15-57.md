# Macro rescanning reaches the caller's token stream

_2026-09-17 15:57:37_

`expand_macros` recursed on each replacement list in isolation, so an expansion
that ended mid-invocation could never be completed: `#define ALIAS IDENTITY`
followed by `ALIAS (2)`, and `#define OPEN ZERO (` followed by `OPEN 1)`, both
left an unexpanded call in the AST where clang folds them. The second shape is
what broke gcc-dg/c23-digit-separators-1.c, whose `b0` expands to `c0 (` with
the argument and closing paren still in the source.

`expand_rescanning` now takes the enclosing stream as `tail` and returns how
many of its tokens the expansion swallowed, so the caller can skip them. A
replacement's invocation may open in the local tokens and close in the tail;
`invocation` splices the two only when the local slice alone has no complete
invocation. Whenever tail tokens are consumed the local tokens are necessarily
exhausted, so the loop always ends right after — which is why one `taken`
counter suffices.

The `disabled` set keeps its insert/remove-around-recursion shape, so blue
paint is unchanged; the fixture asserts that `#define recursive recursive`
still terminates and that a second `ALIAS (20)` on the same line expands after
the first one consumed tail tokens.

Rescanning against the tail requires splicing `rest ++ tail` into a fresh Vec,
which on a long line of object-like macros is O(line) per expansion and
measured ~2x slower. `rescan` now expands the replacement in isolation first —
the common case, and already the final answer — and only redoes it with the
tail when `wants_more` sees an unbalanced `(` or a trailing function-like macro
name. That put the benchmark back on the baseline exactly.

Two pre-existing problems surfaced while benchmarking, both filed rather than
fixed: a long flat expression overflows the stack instead of diagnosing
(slate-parser-87x), and expansion carries an ~80x constant factor against
clang (slate-parser-y5o). Note that timing `slate-parser parse` on macro-heavy
input measures AST pretty-printing — 96% of samples — not the preprocessor;
put the work in a `#if` to isolate expansion.
