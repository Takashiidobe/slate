# Apply the x86-64 large-array alignment rule to array objects

_2026-09-25 21:20:44_

Follow-up to dyd.30: the Rust side needs the 16-byte alignment x86-64 gives large arrays (Slate wraps such arrays in aligned::Align). TargetInfo::large_array_alignment() is 16 on every x86-64 target (clang sets LargeArrayMinWidth/LargeArrayAlign=128 on all x86-64 OSes; gcc's ix86_data_alignment ABI branch agrees). object_alignment_override raises a declared fixed-length array object of >= 16 bytes to max(typed, 16) unless the declaration has aligned/_Alignas; a typedef's own alignment (even on the array typedef, or lowered element typedefs) does not suppress it. Applies to globals, extern declarations, static and automatic locals, arrays of records, and initializer-completed arrays (local alignment is now computed after the initializer completes the type). Not applied to records, incomplete arrays, VLAs (clang's dynamic alloca uses 16, unmodeled), string literals (no entity; clang uses align 1), or compound literals (clang align 4). gcc's extra speed over-alignment (records to 16, >32-byte arrays to 32, char[15] to 8, even at -O0) is not ABI and is not modeled. Verified against clang -emit-llvm on x86_64 linux/windows-msvc/windows-gnu, i386, aarch64, arm32, and gcc asm on x86_64. New fixture large_array_alignment.c; 389 IR fixtures gained [align=16] on large arrays and nothing else changed. ir-spec.md documents the rule.
