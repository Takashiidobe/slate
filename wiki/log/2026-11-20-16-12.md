# slate-8ge7.1 interproc timing: PhaseTimer baseline shows passes near-linear, Rust-AST extraction 20x propagation

_2026-11-20 16:12:17_

- bead: slate-8ge7.1
- kind: feature
- area: crates/slate/src/backend/interproc
- area: tools/benchmark

Outcome: interproc passes have per-phase timers (`SLATE_INTERPROC_TIMING=1`) and a scaling benchmark (`tools/benchmark/interproc_scaling.py`); the baseline shows they are near-linear, and fact extraction from the Rust AST costs about 20x more than propagation.

Gap and why it mattered: slate-8ge7 assumed interproc rewrites were shelved because the passes cost too much. Nothing measured them, and decision 2 of the epic needed time split into (a) extraction, (b) propagation, (c) apply.

Implementation: `interproc::timing::PhaseTimer` laps each phase of `string_params::run`, `pointer_lattice::solve`/`apply`, `length_lattice::apply`/`apply_signature_lifts`, and `interproc::run`. `run_worklist` returns its iteration count. Off unless the env var is set.

Synthetic baseline (N = 200..3200 functions, min of 3 runs): log-log slope of every interproc phase is 0.9 to 1.3 across all ten shapes (chain, -fwrapv chain, kill-at-leaf, kill-at-root, recursive ring, fan-in hub, string chain, buffer chain, 3200 local pointer copies, 3200 pointer fields). Worklist iterations are exactly N or 2N. No shape is clearly super-linear inside interproc. At N=3200 interproc is 35 to 80 ms of 0.5 to 4 s translate wall. Super-linear wall time does appear in `fields` (exp 1.48) and `fanout` (1.19), but outside interproc.

sqlite (translate-project, sqlite3.c + shell.c, 32 s wall): interproc 1.02 s (about 3%).
- extract 840 ms: pointer_lattice validate_plans 353, solve extract 239, length_lattice signatures 108, string_params 69, pairings 7, plus pinned/export planning 65
- propagate 41 ms (pointer 35, string 6, length 0.02); 6656 pointer worklist iterations over 3414 functions
- apply 62 ms, mixed (const_length, return_buffers) 63 ms

Conclusion for the epic's gate: (a) dominates, so per decision 2 the IR form (facts extracted once, not re-derived per pass from Rust spellings) is the lever, not summary-based propagation. Current interproc cost is not a bottleneck in absolute terms; any speed case for the epic rests on planned analyses, not today's three.

Limits: synthetic shapes are single-file `translate` (Exports::Fixed); only sqlite exercises the Liftable merge path. `--sqlite` mode of the script was written but its numbers above come from a direct run with the same command.

See: slate-8ge7, results in tools/benchmark/results/interproc-scaling-2026-11-20.json and interproc-sqlite-2026-11-20.txt.
