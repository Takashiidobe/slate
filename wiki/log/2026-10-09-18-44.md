# #if shifts and ?: types match gcc and clang

_2026-10-09 18:44:28_

slate-parser-cxg.7.7. Two #if arithmetic gaps in const_expr.rs (evaluate_wide, preprocessor path only).

Shift counts: the new Parser::pp_shift follows the flavor. gcc (libcpp reduce) treats a negative count as a shift the other way. For counts >= 64, a left shift gives 0 and a right shift gives the sign fill. clang (PPExpressions) reads the count as unsigned, so a negative count is out of range: a left shift gives 0 and a right shift is clamped to 63, so 0x8000000000000000U >> -1 == 1. msvc keeps its existing mask to 63. Before, every flavor gave 0 for any count that was negative or out of range.

?: type: the result takes the common type of both arms, even the one not evaluated. pp_unsigned decides the type of that arm without evaluating it. It treated comparisons, &&, || and calls (defined) as unsigned, and nested ?: by its condition. It now follows the real rules: comparisons, !, &&, || and defined are signed; arithmetic and bitwise operators are unsigned if either operand is; shifts take the left operand's type; ?: is unsigned if either arm is; a comma takes its right operand's type; a literal is unsigned with a u suffix or when it doesn't fit in intmax_t; a char literal follows the target's char signedness. 13 probes agree with gcc -E and clang -E. Before, 9 of them were wrong.

Migrated gcc.dg/cpp/arith-1.c, if-shift.c and expr.c (gnu23). Added clang/linux/x86_64/pp-if-shift-conditional-type.c, which pins the clang shift and ?: results. The parser profile passes: 7971/7971.
