# Scoped atomic builtins carry a sync_scope through the IR

_2026-09-20 14:34:27_

The `__scoped_atomic_*` family is the `__atomic_*` one with a trailing
synchronization-scope argument. Added `SyncScope`
(system/device/workgroup/wavefront/single/cluster/`dynamic(v)`) and bundled
it with the order as `Atomicity { order, scope }`, which replaces
`MemoryOrder` on `Store`/`Update`/`Read`/`Write`/`Fence`. Bundling rather
than adding a parallel field kept every pass-through site in `effects.rs`
untouched -- those destructure and rebuild `ordering` as one binding.
`CompareExchange` takes a separate `sync_scope`, since its two orderings
share one scope and duplicating it would allow an impossible state.

Printed as `sync_scope=` rather than `scope=`, because `fence` already prints
`scope=thread|signal`, which is unrelated. `SyncScope::System` prints
nothing: it is the default and what every unscoped builtin means, so all
existing fixtures stayed byte-identical and a scoped builtin written with
`__MEMORY_SCOPE_SYSTEM` is indistinguishable from the unscoped one, which is
correct.

The scope is recorded as written, not as the target would lower it. Verified
that clang collapses every scope to the system scope on x86-64 and aarch64
while `amdgcn` gives `syncscope("agent")`/`("workgroup")`/`("wavefront")`/
`("singlethread")`/`("cluster")`; the mapping is the consumer's decision.
A non-constant scope is `dynamic(v)`, which clang turns into a run-time
switch exactly as it does for a non-constant ordering.

`atomic_operation` splits the trailing scope argument off once at the top, so
every scoped spelling works without touching the per-operation arms. The
family has no `signal_fence`, `test_and_set`, `clear`, `init` or lock-free
query, which `scoped_operation` enforces with a positive list.

Grouped `weak`/`form`/`scope` into an `Exchange` struct to keep
`compare_exchange` and `exchange_node` under clippy's argument limit.

Covered by `tests/fixtures/sema/ir_atomic_scoped.c`.
