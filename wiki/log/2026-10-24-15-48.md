# Lower i686 stdcall/fastcall/thiscall where rustc matches clang

_2026-10-24 15:48:22_

slate-00aa: Tables::convention_abi maps X86Stdcall/X86Fastcall/X86Thiscall on TargetFamily::X86 to Abi::StdcallUnwind/FastcallUnwind/ThiscallUnwind (stable rustc ABIs, no feature gate).
Probe (240 signatures per target, clang -emit-llvm vs rustc --emit=llvm-ir, compared by register assignment plus stack bytes, and decorated symbols via llvm-nm): stdcall and thiscall match on i686-pc-windows-msvc and i686-unknown-linux-gnu, records and sret included. fastcall diverges in two classes: an int of at most 32 bits after a long long that found a register free (clang puts it in a register, rustc on the stack), and, off MSVC, record arguments (clang/gcc use registers, rustc byval).
Tables::function_abi requires scalar/native_c shapes and regparm 0 for all x86 conventions (rustc_spells_shapes, generalized from vectorcall); for fastcall, Tables::fastcall_matches_rustc rejects the two classes by type (barrier 'fastcall register assignment rustc diverges on'). Function-pointer types use the same fastcall rule; stdcall/thiscall pointer types get the long-double filter, like win64.
Fixtures: tests/fixtures/i686/x86_conventions.c; tests/fixtures.unsupported/i686/fastcall_divergent.c; tests/fixtures.library/vectorcall_calls (C <-> Rust vectorcall across the boundary on x86_64, closes the gap left by ck8y's static-only fixture). The library harness is x86_64-only, so i686 boundary evidence is the IR probe.
Wiki: calls-abi.md calling-conventions notes corrected (inreg is not always rustc's to get right).
