# Check fixtures with an in-harness matcher instead of FileCheck; revert -SAME splitting

_2026-09-29 09:43:35_

FileCheck compiles every check line that holds a [[...]] or {{...}} into a regex. After
becf64da that meant 263k regex lines, and LLVM's regex engine blows up on long lines and many
captured variables (cpp__embed-14 took 5.5s, bitint-39 3.7s). Rust's regex (benchmarked on the
unsplit becf64da lines) fixed those (21ms, 32ms) but not 20001226-1.c: building a new regex for
each of 16k lines is the cost there (1.67s vs FileCheck's 1.5s).

tests/filecheck/matcher.rs matches the subset the generator emits: plain and -NEXT directives,
[[V:[0-9]+]]/[[V]] and [[#V:]]/[[#V]] variables, and {{[0-9]+}}, {{.*}}, {{\[[0-9, ]+\]}} and
the three escapes. Anything else is an explicit unsupported error. No regex: the pieces are
literals, digit runs and variables. A check must match its whole output line after whitespace
is collapsed, which is stricter than FileCheck. On a failure the harness runs FileCheck for its
dump. Verified by breaking fixtures: a swapped variable use, a swapped goto target, a changed
literal and dropped trailing text all fail.

Removed split_regex_checks from the updater and regenerated the 228 split fixtures; they are
byte-identical to becf64da again. The updater keeps rendering with the release binary.

Suite, runs interleaved on the same machine: 10.1/11.0s now vs 11.8/12.2s at becf64da~1 (before
named variables). The slowest tests (windows_h, float-exact-1) are now bound by rendering.
