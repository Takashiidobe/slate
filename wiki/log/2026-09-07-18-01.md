# printf to print! per-call flush-barrier redesign

_2026-09-07 18:01:10_

Superseded the whole-program all-or-nothing gate from the prior entry
(2026-09-07-17-12) with slate-ato's historical per-call design, per
explicit user direction: this is what they had in mind from the start.
src/backend/engine/printf_format.rs now converts each %d-only printf call
independently; any stdout-writing call it can't convert (non-decimal
format, dynamic format, or any other libc writer: fprintf/puts/fputs/
fwrite/putchar/putc/vprintf/vfprintf, matched by Known identity or by
name against extern decls generated for long-double variadic shims) gets
wrapped with an explicit stdout flush before and libc fflush(NULL) after,
pinning relative order instead of refusing to convert anything.

Two bugs found and fixed while validating against the full corpus:
1) insert_pos for the injected `fflush` extern block mirrored
   prelude::inject (before first Fn/Impl/Static), which landed between a
   struct and its impl block in long_double, breaking an unrelated
   CHECK-NEXT. Fixed to insert after the last existing ExternBlock.
2) A raw stdout-writer call that happens to be a function's tail
   expression (e.g. `ret = vprintf(...); return ret;` collapsed to tail
   position, gcc-torture's printf-chk-1/vprintf-chk-1/etc.) was silently
   losing its return value: appending the post-call fflush after it stole
   the tail-expression slot. Fixed via wrap_stdout_writer_preserving_tail,
   which binds the call to a temp and re-emits the temp as the new tail.

~250 fixtures needed mechanical FileCheck regen (broader than the prior
entry's ~160, since flush-wrapping now covers the whole stdio-writer
family, not just printf). Three fixtures whose AArch64-GNU translation
crashes clang/CIR pre-existingly (BitInt>128, an unrelated frontend bug)
were regenerated with --target X86_64-GNU=... to avoid the tool's
all-or-nothing target write.
