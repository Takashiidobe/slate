Outcome: the asm `%a` modifier on immediate operands now lowers on i686, x86 Intel dialect, aarch64 and arm, and on x86_64 symbols under `-masm=intel`. Only i686 symbols remain a barrier, and they have their own bead.

## Gap and why it mattered

slate-f2zu.14.3 lowered `%a` on symbol and immediate operands for x86_64 AT&T only. Everywhere else the result was `symbol modifier Some('a')` or `immediate modifier Some('a')`. `%a` on a register already lowered on every target.

## Oracle behavior (gcc / clang, -O2)

| Target | immediate `"i"(8)` | symbol `"i"(&arr[2])` |
|---|---|---|
| x86_64 Intel | gcc `ds:8`, clang cannot assemble | gcc `"arr"[rip+16]`, clang cannot assemble |
| i686 AT&T | `8` | `arr+16`, but only with `-fno-pic`; both reject it under PIC |
| aarch64 | clang `#8` | gcc rejects `"i"(&sym)` |
| arm | clang `#8`, gcc `8` | not checked |

## Before / After

    long v; __asm__("lea %0, %a1" : "=r"(v) : "i"(8));            /* -masm=intel */
    long w; __asm__("mov %0, QWORD PTR %a1" : "=r"(w) : "i"(&arr[2]));

Before: `immediate modifier Some('a')` and `symbol modifier Some('a')`.

After:

    "lea {0:r}, [{1}]", lateout(reg) __t0, const 8 as i32
    "mov {0:r}, QWORD PTR [rip + {1}+16]", lateout(reg) __t0, sym arr
    aarch64: "mov {0:x}, #{1}", const 8

## Implementation

In crates/slate/src/frontend/lowerer/asm.rs:
- `asm_template`: immediate `%a` prints `[{N}]` under Intel, `#{N}` on non-x86 targets, and `{N}` otherwise. The `x86_64_att` binding is gone.
- `symbol_reference`: allows `%a` on any x86_64 dialect and wraps the reference as `[rip + ...]` under Intel or `...(%rip)` under AT&T.

## Fixtures

- crates/slate/tests/fixtures/gcc/x86_64/asm_intel_address_modifier.c (`-masm=intel`, gcc oracle; covers symbol, symbol+offset, lea of a symbol, immediate, register)
- crates/slate/tests/fixtures/{aarch64,arm,i686}/asm_address_modifier_immediate.c (immediate and register `%a`)

Limits: an i686 symbol under `%a`, and the existing `$sym` form, need a non-PIC Rust build. Rust's i686 PIE link fails with "R_386_32 cannot be used against symbol". That work is slate-3f8g.4.43.
