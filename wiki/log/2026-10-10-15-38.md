# Lex digraphs everywhere but strict C89, keeping their spelling

_2026-10-10 15:38:33_

slate-parser-cxg.7.15.

The lexer had no digraphs at all. It now lexes <: :> <% %> %: %:%: as [ ] { } # ## when StandardFeatures::digraphs is set, so %: starts a directive and is the stringify operator, and %:%: pastes. Probed against gcc and clang: on in every mode except -std=c89/c90 (on in iso9899:199409 and gnu89, and for msvc, verified with cl /EP). clang's -fdigraphs/-fno-digraphs override (Opt::ClangDigraphs); gcc rejects both. Trigraph and digraph overrides now travel together as CompilerOptions::graphs (SourceGraphs).

Spelling: gcc and clang stringify a digraph with its own spelling (STR(<:) is "<:"). Tokens carry Span::digraph next to leading_space, and lexer::token_spelling maps it back. Stringify (stringized_source), ## re-lex (paste, which also keeps the flag of the pasted token, so GLUE(<, :) stringifies as "<:") and pp printing use it.

Fixtures: {gcc,clang}/linux/x86_64/pp_digraphs.c (C99, C94, gnu89; clang adds c89 -fdigraphs), error/gcc digraphs-c89.c and fdigraphs-clang-only.c, error/clang digraphs-disabled.c. Migrated gcc.dg cpp/digraph1.c, cpp/digraphs.c, cpp/paste2.c. suites/gcc-dg/{gcc,clang} c94-digraph-1.c and c99-digraph-1.c had encoded the old output ("1 %:%: 2"), now "12" as the tests require. pp output in c89 still prints '< :' because would_paste spaces any punctuator pair (pre-existing, cosmetic).
