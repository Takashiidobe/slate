# GNU builtin setjmp/longjmp and __int128 va_arg (slate-oreg.10)

_2026-10-18 14:49:24_

Promoted gcc-torture pr64242 and pr92904.

pr64242: __builtin_setjmp/__builtin_longjmp were linked straight to libc setjmp/longjmp. The GNU buffer is 5 words and may be copied (the test memcpys it and longjmps from the copy); glibc's 200-byte jmp_buf overflowed it and the copy dropped the saved rsp/pc. Now __builtin_setjmp(buf) hoists a 512-byte, 16-aligned __slate_builtin_setjmp::Buffer local in the caller's frame, stores its address in buf[0], and calls _setjmp on it in place. __builtin_longjmp(buf, v) calls _longjmp(*buf[0], v). _setjmp/_longjmp match the builtins in not saving the signal mask. Support module: frontend/support/builtin_setjmp.rs.

pr92904: va_arg(ap, __int128) emitted next_arg::<i128>(), which needs the unstable c_variadic_int128. Plain 128-bit integers now take the existing wide-int path (Tables::wide_va_arg): on x86_64 that is __slate_sysv_va::arg with classes 0b0101, and on aarch64 it is __slate_aapcs_va::gp. Second bug: the sysv helper capped overflow-area alignment at 16, but clang aligns to the type's real alignment (32 for an aligned(32) struct, the subject of PR 92904). It now uses align_of::<T>().max(8).

FileCheck regenerated for bitint_va_wide, va_arg_records, x86_64/va_arg_aggregates (helper line only). slate profile otherwise green.
