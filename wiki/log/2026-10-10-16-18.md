# Preprocessor macro-redefined, builtin-macro-redefined and deprecated warnings

_2026-10-10 16:18:33_

slate-parser-cxg.7.21: slate-parser had no named preprocessor warning, so -Werror=builtin-macro-redefined, -Werror=deprecated and -pedantic-errors accepted code gcc and clang reject.

Preprocessor::warn resolves a Warning through DiagnosticContext and records a DirectiveDiagnostic (new warning field, shown as the -W code); directives in system headers are skipped, which keeps slate's predefine files quiet. DiagnosticContext::severity_as takes pedantic membership explicitly.

Rules (pp/define.rs redefinition_warning), from probing gcc 16, clang 22, cl 19.51:
- different definition (tokens, whitespace presence, parameter names, object vs function): gcc unnamed 'X redefined', clang -Wmacro-redefined, cl C4005 (parameter renames ignored). All under macro-redefined.
- gcc: __TIME__/__DATE__/__FILE__/__FILE_NAME__/__BASE_FILE__/__TIMESTAMP__ are builtin-macro-redefined; other builtins, __has_* and any defined __STDC_* name (bar FORMAT/LIMIT/CONSTANT_MACROS) warn unnamed even when identical, #undef too. gcc #undef warnings are not pedantic.
- clang: special builtins and predefined __STDC* macros are builtin-macro-redefined for #define and #undef.
- msvc: its reserved builtins give C4117 (still ignored) as builtin-macro-redefined.
- gcc #assert/#unassert and each #pred test in #if raise deprecated.

Slate matches gcc and clang on 1728 of 1744 probe runs; the rest are the pre-existing 'defined' macro name acceptance (filed slate-parser-cxg.7.26). Also filed .25 (-Wtraditional, split out) and .27 (msvc reserved __STDC_VERSION__).
