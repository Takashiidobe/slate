# flag isolation on clang-cir blowup: no divergence below the cliff

_2026-08-23 11:27:13_

Follow-up on slate-wcf7.2 (clang-cir emit-cir superlinear blowup). Tested whether any slate-side flags (-Xclang -disable-llvm-passes, -std=gnu23, -nostdlibinc+libc-shim isystem, custom \__SLATE_\* defines, explicit -target) contribute to the blowup, vs a minimal 'clang -fclangir -emit-cir' invocation with default host target/headers. At 19698 lines (below the cliff) all variants completed in ~7.1-7.2s, no measurable difference. Could not test at the size where the problem actually manifests (~60K+ lines) within a short timeout, since even the known-good full-flags case takes ~80s just to abort there -- a short timeout can't distinguish normal compilation from runaway growth at that size. Conclusion: no evidence any slate flag is the driver; behavior is identical below the cliff, so the blowup most likely tracks input size/shape (macro density, function/node count) rather than flag choice. Useful context for the upstream ClangIR report.
